# Introduction
Learning new skills is how most of us bring new ideas to life, move our careers forward and keep work interesting. Nobody wants to stand still.

But it is hard to know where to start, both for yourself and for a team. Are juniors getting enough support? Which tools are worth the time? Is it better to go deep in one craft or broad across several?

We surveyed 1,200 people at more than 80 studios, from design to film, and asked what they learn, how they learn it and what they wish they had been taught.

Over the next pages we look at five themes: the skills that matter most, the ones in demand now, how managers support learning, which new tools people are adopting, and the old question of specialist versus generalist.

# 1 Foundational skills
We started at the very beginning of a creative career and asked which skills are the ideal foundation. Visual design came out on top, followed by image editing and layout. Most people are teaching themselves these skills, and few think school prepared them for the tools they use daily.

### Learning at work
# What managers can do
- **Protect learning time** — A fixed half-day a month beats good intentions
- **Pair juniors with leads** — Most learning happens next to someone better
- **Pay for courses** — Only 1 in 3 studios offers a budget today
- **Share what works** — Short internal demos spread new skills fastest
