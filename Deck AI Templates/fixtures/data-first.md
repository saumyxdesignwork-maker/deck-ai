### Operations review · 2026
# *Clinic* Flow
Where patient time goes, and how we get it back
October 2026

---

# 01 What we measured
Twelve city clinics, ninety days, every visit timed end to end.

---

### A — The study
# How we score a clinic
Each clinic gets a flow score from 38 timed checkpoints, grouped into six areas.
- **Arrival and check-in**
- **Triage speed**
- **Waiting-room load**
- **Consult hand-off**
- **Prescription turnaround**
- **Follow-up booking**

---

### Patient research
# What patients told us
- **62%** Waited longer than they were told
- **41%** Left without booking a follow-up {emphasis}
- **78%** Would use live queue updates

> Exit survey, n = 2,140 patients

---

### Demand
# *Why this matters.* The waiting room is the product most patients actually experience.
Most of a visit is time spent waiting, not time with a clinician. Small gains at the front desk compound across a city.
- **1.2M** Visits a year across the network
- **640K** Visits at the six busiest clinics
- **210K** Visits that breach the 45-minute wait target
- **~48K** Visits where patients leave before being seen {emphasis}

### Visit anatomy
# How a visit *moves*
A typical visit passes through five stages; most delay builds before the consult.
- **Step 1** Arrive — Kiosk or desk check-in
- **Step 2** Triage — Nurse assessment
- **Step 3** Wait — Queue for a room {emphasis}
- **Step 4** Consult — Time with clinician
- **Step 5** Follow-up — Booking and prescriptions

### Principles
# What we will not trade away
- **Clinical safety first** — No speed target overrides a nurse's judgement
- **Honest waiting times** — Show the real queue, even when it is long
- **Fewer forms, not faster typing** — Remove steps before automating them
